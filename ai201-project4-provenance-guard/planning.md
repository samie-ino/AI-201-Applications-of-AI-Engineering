# Provenance Guard: Architecture & Implementation Plan

## 1. Detection Signals
The detection pipeline uses two distinct signals to evaluate text.

*   **Signal 1: Text Predictability (Perplexity)**
    *   **What it measures:** The statistical likelihood of the text's word choices based on standard language models.
    *   **Output:** A normalized score from `0.0` (highly predictable) to `1.0` (highly unpredictable).
    *   **Interpretation:** AI models output highly probable words (closer to 0.0). Humans use quirkier, less predictable vocabulary (closer to 1.0).
*   **Signal 2: Sentence Variation (Burstiness)**
    *   **What it measures:** The variance in sentence lengths and structural complexity throughout the text.
    *   **Output:** A normalized score from `0.0` (highly uniform) to `1.0` (highly varied).
    *   **Interpretation:** AI models write with rhythmic, uniform sentence structures (closer to 0.0). Humans write in "bursts" of short and long sentences (closer to 1.0).
*   **Combination Method:** The system calculates a weighted average of both signals (e.g., 50% Predictability, 50% Burstiness) to produce a single raw confidence score between `0.0` and `1.0`.

## 2. Uncertainty Representation
A confidence score is not a binary switch. It represents the statistical likelihood of human authorship. 
*   **What a 0.60 score means:** The text leans slightly toward human authorship, but it lacks the strong variance and unpredictability usually found in organic writing. The system is uncertain.
*   **Thresholds:**
    *   `0.00` to `0.35`: **Likely AI** (Strong uniformity and predictability)
    *   `0.36` to `0.65`: **Uncertain** (Mixed signals or neutral structure)
    *   `0.66` to `1.00`: **Likely Human** (Strong variation and unpredictability)

## 3. Transparency Label Design
The system will output one of three distinct labels based on the final confidence score, designed to be easily understood by non-technical users.

*   **High-Confidence AI (Score 0.00 - 0.35):** 
    *"This content was likely generated by an AI tool. Our systems detected highly predictable language patterns and uniform sentence structures typical of machine generation."*
*   **Uncertain (Score 0.36 - 0.65):** 
    *"This content shows mixed signals. It contains structural patterns common to both human writers and AI tools, so we cannot make a definitive classification."*
*   **High-Confidence Human (Score 0.66 - 1.00):** 
    *"This content was likely written by a human. Our systems detected natural variations in sentence structure and vocabulary."*

## 4. Appeals Workflow
*   **Who can appeal:** The authenticated creator/author of the submitted content.
*   **Information provided:** The original `submission_id` and a text field for `creator_reasoning` (e.g., explaining why their specific writing style might have triggered a false positive).
*   **System Action:** Upon receiving the `POST /appeal`, the system updates the submission's status from `"Final"` to `"Under Review"`. It logs the appeal payload alongside the original decision in the Audit Log.
*   **Reviewer View:** A human reviewer opening the appeal queue will see: the original raw text, the individual scores for Predictability and Burstiness, the overall confidence score, the label that was issued, and the creator's written reasoning.

## 5. Anticipated Edge Cases
*   **Edge Case 1: The Technical Manual.** A human author writes a highly structured instructional document, legal brief, or standard operating procedure. Because the text relies on standardized terminology (low predictability) and methodical, uniform formatting (low burstiness), the system will likely misclassify this human-written text as AI-generated.
*   **Edge Case 2: Heavily Prompted AI.** A user prompts an AI with explicit instructions: *"Write this with lots of slang, use varied sentence lengths, and include occasional grammatical mistakes."* This forces the AI to output high burstiness and high unpredictability, successfully tricking the heuristics into scoring the text as "Likely Human."
